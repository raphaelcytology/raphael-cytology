Overview

CLIA'88 requires that all laboratories use "descriptive" terminology for reporting Pap test results. Terminology must be unambiguous and clinically relevent.

Reporting Systems

Papanicolaou Classification System (1950s)

  • Class I- Normal, benign cells
  • Class II- Minor cell changes consistent with benign conditions
  • Class III- Cells suspicious, but not truly diagnostic, of malignancy
  • Class IV- Cells fairly conclusive of malignancy
  • Class V- Diagnostic malignant cells

Cervical Intraepithelial Neoplasia (CIN)

  • CIN 1 (Mild dysplasia)- Abnormal growth in the lower third of squamous epithelium
  • CIN 2 (Moderate dysplasia)- Abnormal growth between one and two thirds of squamous epithelium
  • CIN 3 (Severe / CIS)- Anormal growth in more than two thirds of the squamous epithelium

The Bethesda Classification System (1991)

  • National Cancer Institute convened in Bethesda, Maryland in 1988 to standardize Pap reporting
  • First published in 1991, now updated every 10 years
  • Main purpose was to:
    • Create uniform diagnostic terminology
    • Base classification on current understanding of disease process
    • Include specimen adequacy as part of report
    • Eliminate the Class System
  • Categories are unambiguous and enable clinicians to triage patients to next course of action
  • Four categories:
    1. Adequacy of specimen
    2. General categorization
    3. Descriptive diagnosis
    4. Hormonal evaluation

Adequacy of Specimen

  • Considered the single most important QA component of TBS
  • Must meet the following criteria:
    • Correct specimen identification- patient name must match requisition
    • Pertinent clinical information- age, LMP, GPA, use of hormones etc.
    • Must be able to interpret cells- visible and well preserved
    • Samples must contain cells from squamocolumnar junction
  • 1991 TBS adequacy has three categories:
    1. Satisfactory for evaluation
      • Identifiers match
      • Well preserved and visualized squamous cells cover 10% of slide
      • Two clusters of endocervical cells (5 cells each)
    2. Satisfactory but limited by...
      • Lack of age or LMP
      • Obscurance of 50-75% of epithelial cells by blood, inflammatory cells, etc.
      • Lack of endocervical component
    3. Unsatisfactory for evaluation
      • No patient identifiers
      • Broken slide
      • Poor cell preservation or obscuring of more than 75% of slide
  • If specimen has abnormal cells IT IS NEVER UNSAT

General Categorization

  • Most imortant category in TBS because it helps clinicians triage cases
  • Three types of categories:
    • Within Normal Limits (WNL)
    • Benign Cellular Changes (BCC)
    • Epithelial Cell Abnormality (ECA)
  • BCC and ECA require further information in Descriptive Diagnoses Category

Descriptive Diagnosis

  • BCC and ECA needed to be further explained
  • Benign changes included infection organisms and reactive changes
  • ECA includes all precursor lesions and malignant changes
  • Subdivided into Squamous and Glandular lesions
  • Squamous Cell abnormalities:
    1. ASCUS
    2. Low grade SIL
    3. High Grade SIL
    4. Squamous Cell Carcinoma
  • Glandular cell abnormalities:
    1. Endometrial cells, cytologically benign in a postmenopaussal woman
    2. AGUS
    3. Endometrial Adenocarcinoma
    4. Endocervical Adenocarcinoma
    5. Extrauterine Adenocarcinoma (spread to uterus from another site)
    6. Adenocarcinoma, NOS
  • Abnormalities not classified as squamous or glandular are classified under OTHER
  • These are rare tumors such as lymphoma, melanoma, sarcoma, etc.

Hormonal Evaluation

  • Some laboratories only report hormonal evaluation if pattern is not consistent with patient's age and/or history
  • Evaluation:
    1. Hormonal pattern is consistent with age and history
    2. Hormonal pattern inconsistent with age and history (elevated or decreased)
    3. Hormonal pattern assessment not possible due to organism, lack of LMP, obscuring factor, etc.

The Bethesda System (2001)

  • "Satisfactory but limited" category eliminated
  • Adequate number of squamous cells "10% of slide" was redefined to "estimated number of squamous cells"
    • For conventional: 8000-12,000 squamous cells
    • For LBC: Minimum 5000 squamous cells
    • For estimating adequacy, count fields across diamter of LBC circles
    • For ThinPrep, with 10x objective count 100 fields, each field should contain 50 cells
    • For SurePath, with 10x objective count 36 fields, each field should contain 118 cells
  • Ten endocervical/metaplastic cells in groups or singly are required
  • Interpretation/results category was introduced
  • Within Normal Limits and Benign Cellular Changes combined to form Negative for Intraepithelial Lesion or Malignancy (NILM)
  • General category is now optional
  • ASCUS was divided to ASCUS and ASC-H
  • Addition of "Glandular cells status post-hysterectomy" was added
  • "Automated review", "Ancillary testing", and "Educational notes" were added to be used as needed
  • Hormonal evaluation no longer has to be performed

The Bethesda System (2014)

  • Very minimal terminology changes
  • Changes:
    • Documenting "Benign appearing endometrial cells" is recommended in women over 45 years old
    • Educational notes comment that endometrial evaluations should be done in PM women
    • "Adjunctive testing" replaces Ancillary testing
    • "Computer-Assisted Interpretation of Cervical Cytology" replaces automated testing

Example Reports

Example 1

  • TBS 1991
  • A pap smear taken from a woman with a cervix shows no endocervical/metaplastic cells, but is normal
  • Report
  • Specimen adequacy: Sat but limited by lack of endocervical cells
  • General Category: WNL
  • Hormonal pattern consistent with age and history

Example 2

  • TBS 1991
  • A technically satisfactory cervical/vaginal smear shows cells consistent with repair and contains Trichomonas.
  • Report
  • Specimen adequacy: Satisfactory for Evaluation
  • General Category: Benign Cellular Changes
  • Descriptive Diagnosis: Reparative endocervical cell changes. Trichomonas vaginalis organisms
  • Hormonal evaluation not performed due to presence of Trichomonas vaginalis

Example 3

  • TBS 2001
  • Normal sample
  • Report
  • Specimen adequacy: Satisfactory for Evaluation; transformation zone component present
  • Interpretation: Negative for Intraepithelial Lesion or Malignancy

Example 4

  • TBS 2001
  • A technical satisfactory slide reveals cells consistent with endocervical adenocarcinoma.
  • Report
  • Specimen adequacy: Satisfactory for Evaluation
  • Results: Endocervical adenocarcinoma
